# THE LAST ASSESSMENT

### Complete Story Script — Questions, Conversations & Endings

**Genre:** Psychological sci-fi mystery
**Structure:** Prologue, five acts, 20 questions, six endings
**Setting:** Eterna, a society where human memories can be recorded, valued, transferred, and restored.

---

# PROLOGUE — POST-DEATH ASSESSMENT

A white room. The protagonist sits at a desk. A glass wall overlooks a quiet city.

**ASSESSOR:** Welcome. Your death has been confirmed, and your memories have been preserved for evaluation.

**PROTAGONIST:** So… this is what happens after you die?

**ASSESSOR:** In your case, yes. Before your memories can be processed, we must assess their continuity and integrity.

**PROTAGONIST:** And what happens after that?

**ASSESSOR:** Your results will determine the next stage.

A screen lights up.

**PRELIMINARY VALUATION: 143 CREDITS**

**PROTAGONIST:** What are credits supposed to mean?

**ASSESSOR:** Your assessment will clarify your result. Shall we begin?

The protagonist hesitates, then nods.

**ASSESSOR:** There are twenty questions. Some concern your memories, while others concern your decisions and experiences. Please answer as accurately as you can.

**PROTAGONIST:** And if I can't remember something?

**ASSESSOR:** Uncertainty is an acceptable response.

The first question appears.

---

# ACT I — INTAKE

*The assessment appears routine. The protagonist learns about the supposed post-death process while small details begin to feel strangely familiar.*

## QUESTION 1 — Familiarity

**ASSESSOR:** People sometimes recognise places or voices without remembering where they encountered them. Has that happened to you?

A. Yes, quite often.
B. Occasionally, but I usually remember eventually.
C. Rarely. I trust what I remember.
D. Lately, more often than I'd like.

**PROTAGONIST:** Is that normal after death?

**ASSESSOR:** Memory recall can vary during the preservation process.

**PROTAGONIST:** So I might remember things later?

**ASSESSOR:** That is possible.

**PROTAGONIST:** Good. There are a few things I'd like to remember.

The Assessor records the response.

**ASSESSOR:** Understood. Let us continue.

---

## QUESTION 2 — The Memory Worth Keeping

Four images appear on the screen: a hand repairing a broken clock, two people embracing at a railway station, a technical diagram covered in corrections, and an employee badge with a scratched photograph.

**ASSESSOR:** These images represent different types of experiences. Which would you consider most valuable to preserve?

A. The clock repair. It represents a skill that could be passed on.
B. The embrace. Some experiences matter because of the people involved.
C. The diagram. Knowledge can benefit many people.
D. The damaged badge. Even unpleasant experiences may be worth remembering.

The protagonist studies the images.

**PROTAGONIST:** Are these from real people's memories?

**ASSESSOR:** They are anonymised samples.

**PROTAGONIST:** Even the badge?

**ASSESSOR:** Yes.

The protagonist looks at the scratched photograph.

**PROTAGONIST:** I feel like I've seen that before.

**ASSESSOR:** Familiarity has been noted. Would you like to elaborate?

**PROTAGONIST:** No. I can't place it.

The image disappears.

**ASSESSOR:** That is acceptable. We will proceed.

---

## QUESTION 3 — The Valuation

The screen displays the number again.

**143 CREDITS**

**ASSESSOR:** Your recorded experiences have been assigned a preliminary value of 143 credits. Comparable profiles typically receive a higher score. How would you interpret this difference?

A. My experiences may not have been as significant as I believed.
B. The scoring system may value certain experiences more than others.
C. Something in my record may be missing or incorrectly classified.
D. I would need to understand the criteria before drawing a conclusion.

**PROTAGONIST:** Is that a good score?

**ASSESSOR:** It is below the current average for comparable profiles.

**PROTAGONIST:** Comparable to mine in what way?

**ASSESSOR:** Age, recorded experience, and memory integrity.

**PROTAGONIST:** I don't remember much about my life. How can you know whether it's comparable?

**ASSESSOR:** Your archived records provide information that you may not currently recall.

**PROTAGONIST:** Right. I suppose that's the point of this.

The number shrinks into the corner. For a brief moment, another figure appears beneath it before vanishing.

**PROTAGONIST:** Was there another number?

**ASSESSOR:** No additional value is currently displayed.

The protagonist looks at the screen for another second, then continues.

---

## QUESTION 4 — An Old Voice

A faint waveform appears.

**ASSESSOR:** Some people remember voices more clearly than faces. Is there a voice from your earlier life that you remember particularly well?

A. Someone speaking softly, as though trying not to be heard.
B. Someone calling for me from another room.
C. A voice that feels familiar, though I cannot identify it.
D. No particular voice comes to mind.

A faint sound plays.

**VOICE:** You promised—

The recording cuts off.

**PROTAGONIST:** What was that?

**ASSESSOR:** An audio fragment associated with the memory category you selected.

**PROTAGONIST:** It sounded real.

**ASSESSOR:** Reconstructed memories may contain recorded material as reference.

**PROTAGONIST:** Can I hear it again?

**ASSESSOR:** The fragment is unavailable for repeated playback in this section.

**PROTAGONIST:** Convenient.

**ASSESSOR:** Would you like to report a concern?

**PROTAGONIST:** No. It probably doesn't mean anything.

The waveform disappears.

The protagonist watches the empty screen for a moment before moving on.

---

# ACT II — THE LIFE YOU LIVED

*The questions turn towards the protagonist's previous employment and relationships. The memories feel increasingly personal, although the protagonist doesn't yet know why.*

## QUESTION 5 — Professional Judgment

**ASSESSOR:** Your previous employment involved reviewing recorded experiences and determining whether they met the criteria for preservation. Which part of that work did you consider most important?

A. Applying rules consistently, regardless of personal opinion.
B. Considering the people whose experiences were being reviewed.
C. Identifying errors and improving the accuracy of records.
D. Understanding the consequences of a decision before making it.

**PROTAGONIST:** I was a memory auditor?

**ASSESSOR:** That is the title listed in your employment record.

**PROTAGONIST:** I knew that. I just wanted to hear it said aloud.

**ASSESSOR:** Your evaluations were consistently satisfactory.

**PROTAGONIST:** Was I good at the job?

**ASSESSOR:** Your performance met the required standards.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's a very official way of saying yes.

**ASSESSOR:** It is an accurate way of describing your record.

A short employment summary appears. The protagonist notices a name beside an old project entry.

**PROTAGONIST:** Who's that?

**ASSESSOR:** The name belongs to a former colleague.

**PROTAGONIST:** I don't remember them.

**ASSESSOR:** That is not unusual during memory assessment.

The summary closes before the protagonist can examine it further.

---

## QUESTION 6 — An Unfinished Promise

**ASSESSOR:** Unfinished obligations can remain memorable long after the surrounding events have faded. Do you recall a promise you struggled to keep?

A. I did what I could, even when it became difficult.
B. I failed someone, and I still regret it.
C. I found another way to fulfil the promise.
D. I cannot confidently recall a specific example.

The protagonist selects an answer.

The voice from the earlier recording returns faintly.

**VOICE:** You promised you wouldn't let them take it.

**PROTAGONIST:** There it is again.

**ASSESSOR:** Could you repeat that?

**PROTAGONIST:** I heard the same voice.

**ASSESSOR:** Repeated experiences can occur during memory reconstruction.

**PROTAGONIST:** Does that mean it's a real memory?

**ASSESSOR:** Not necessarily.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then how do I know?

**ASSESSOR:** That is one purpose of this assessment.

The protagonist looks down at the desk.

**PROTAGONIST:** I think I let someone down.

**ASSESSOR:** Do you remember who?

**PROTAGONIST:** No.

The Assessor records the response and moves to the next question.

---

## QUESTION 7 — The Empty Desk

A reconstructed office appears. One desk has been cleared, leaving a faint rectangular mark on its surface.

**ASSESSOR:** Imagine returning to work after an absence. A colleague's desk has been cleared. Your supervisor says the colleague resigned several weeks earlier. What would you be most likely to do?

A. Accept the explanation unless something contradicts it.
B. Ask colleagues whether they knew about the resignation.
C. Try to remember the last time I spoke to the colleague.
D. Look for a reason the departure might have been kept quiet.

The protagonist examines the desk.

**PROTAGONIST:** I know this place.

**ASSESSOR:** The reconstruction is based on your former workplace.

**PROTAGONIST:** I remember someone sitting there. They used to tap their fingers on the desk all the time.

**ASSESSOR:** Can you remember their name?

**PROTAGONIST:** No. But I remember being annoyed by it.

**ASSESSOR:** Emotional details can persist when other information becomes inaccessible.

**PROTAGONIST:** I wish I could remember their face.

The reconstruction fades away.

For a moment, the protagonist seems reluctant to continue.

**ASSESSOR:** Would you like a short pause?

**PROTAGONIST:** No. Let's finish.

---

## QUESTION 8 — Looking Back

**ASSESSOR:** If you could revisit one period of your life with the knowledge you have now, what would you hope to change?

A. I would have worked harder to achieve my goals.
B. I would have spent more time with people who mattered to me.
C. I would have questioned decisions I accepted too easily.
D. I would have tried to understand events before they became irreversible.

**PROTAGONIST:** Does this affect my credits?

**ASSESSOR:** It contributes to your behavioural profile but does not directly alter your current valuation.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then what does affect it?

**ASSESSOR:** The valuation reflects the eligible experiences in your archived profile.

**PROTAGONIST:** And if an experience isn't in the archive?

**ASSESSOR:** It cannot contribute to the current valuation.

The protagonist pauses.

**PROTAGONIST:** Even if it mattered?

**ASSESSOR:** The valuation is based on recorded information.

The next question appears before the protagonist can respond.

---

# ACT III — MISSING RECORDS

*The protagonist starts noticing that the official records don't fully match their recollections. Eterna continues to offer reasonable explanations, but the gaps are becoming harder to ignore.*

## QUESTION 9 — The Familiar Corridor

A reconstruction of the protagonist's former workplace appears.

**ASSESSOR:** Please examine the image. Does anything appear inconsistent with your recollection?

A. The reconstruction seems accurate.
B. Something seems to be missing, though I cannot identify it.
C. One part looks different from what I remember.
D. I cannot recall the place clearly enough to compare it.

The protagonist studies the corridor.

**PROTAGONIST:** There should be another door.

**ASSESSOR:** Which door?

**PROTAGONIST:** At the end. Just past the storage room.

**ASSESSOR:** The available floor plan contains no additional door at that location.

**PROTAGONIST:** Maybe I'm remembering another floor.

The protagonist approaches the blank wall in the reconstruction.

**PROTAGONIST:** I remember going through it.

**ASSESSOR:** Your recollection has been noted.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's all?

**ASSESSOR:** Would you like to record a discrepancy?

The protagonist hesitates.

**PROTAGONIST:** Not yet. I might be wrong.

The reconstruction closes.

The protagonist continues looking at the spot where the door should have been.

---

## QUESTION 10 — A Difficult Decision

**ASSESSOR:** During your employment, you may have encountered information that could benefit others but was not approved for general access. Which consideration should guide a decision about such information?

A. The rules governing its use.
B. The potential harm caused by withholding it.
C. Whether the people affected had consented to its use.
D. Whether the information has been properly understood and verified.

**PROTAGONIST:** Did I have access to restricted records?

**ASSESSOR:** Your role required several levels of access.

**PROTAGONIST:** And did I ever do anything with them?

**ASSESSOR:** Your employment summary does not include a complete record of every action.

**PROTAGONIST:** Is that normal?

**ASSESSOR:** Not every operational event is included in a summary.

A line appears at the bottom of the screen:

*ARCHIVE REFERENCE: PARTIAL.*

**PROTAGONIST:** What's that?

**ASSESSOR:** A notice concerning the available records.

**PROTAGONIST:** Can you show me the full record?

**ASSESSOR:** The original source is unavailable in this assessment.

The notice disappears.

**PROTAGONIST:** Right. Of course it is.

---

## QUESTION 11 — A Gap in the Timeline

The protagonist's employment history appears on screen. Several years contain detailed entries. Then the entries become sparse.

Three years and four months are represented by a single line.

**ASSESSOR:** Your employment record contains fewer entries during this period. Your position and salary appear unchanged. Which explanation seems most plausible?

A. My work continued normally, but fewer records were preserved.
B. I was assigned duties that were recorded elsewhere.
C. Something changed during this period without being documented properly.
D. I cannot judge without remembering what happened.

**PROTAGONIST:** How long is that gap?

**ASSESSOR:** Three years and four months.

**PROTAGONIST:** And I kept working?

**ASSESSOR:** Your employment status remained active.

The protagonist studies the timeline.

**PROTAGONIST:** I remember starting the job. I remember getting promoted. But those years are almost empty.

**ASSESSOR:** The available records do not establish why.

**PROTAGONIST:** Could I have been on leave?

**ASSESSOR:** There is no recorded leave during that period.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then what was I doing?

**ASSESSOR:** I cannot determine that from the available information.

The protagonist looks at the timeline again.

**PROTAGONIST:** I thought you had all my memories.

**ASSESSOR:** I have access to the records provided for this assessment.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's not quite the same thing, is it?

The Assessor does not answer immediately.

**ASSESSOR:** No. It is not.

---

## QUESTION 12 — Residual Audio

An old audio file appears. Its metadata is incomplete.

**ASSESSOR:** An archived audio fragment is available. Its original context was not retained. Would you like to listen to it?

A. Play the complete available fragment.
B. Review its metadata before listening.
C. Preserve it and continue the assessment.
D. Skip the fragment.

If the player chooses to listen, a voice emerges through static.

**VOICE:** If you're hearing this, they've already found my records. I thought I had more time.

A metallic sound follows. The recording ends.

**PROTAGONIST:** Who was that?

**ASSESSOR:** The recording does not contain a verified speaker identification.

**PROTAGONIST:** It sounds like the person from the other memory.

**ASSESSOR:** Similarity between voices is not sufficient to establish identity.

**PROTAGONIST:** Can you check when it was recorded?

**ASSESSOR:** The available metadata is incomplete.

**PROTAGONIST:** Where did it come from?

**ASSESSOR:** The original source is not available in this assessment.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's a lot of things that aren't available.

**ASSESSOR:** I understand your concern.

**PROTAGONIST:** Do you?

**ASSESSOR:** I can record that you have raised it.

The protagonist stares at the file.

**PROTAGONIST:** Keep it. I might need it later.

The recording is added to the assessment's available evidence.

---

# ACT IV — FORBIDDEN TRUTH

*The protagonist begins connecting the missing records to their former work. Their final night becomes increasingly difficult to reconcile with Eterna's explanation.*

## QUESTION 13 — Exceptions

**ASSESSOR:** Rules provide consistency, but unusual circumstances may require exceptions. When, if ever, should a person act against an established procedure?

A. When following it would cause greater harm.
B. Only when the exception can be justified openly.
C. When the procedure itself is clearly failing its purpose.
D. Only after the available evidence has been reviewed.

**PROTAGONIST:** I used to approve these records, didn't I?

**ASSESSOR:** Your role included reviewing and approving eligible memory records.

**PROTAGONIST:** What if a record was approved incorrectly?

**ASSESSOR:** The system provides procedures for correction.

**PROTAGONIST:** And if the system itself was wrong?

**ASSESSOR:** Errors can occur at multiple stages of a process.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's not really an answer.

**ASSESSOR:** It is the most accurate answer I can provide.

The protagonist looks at the authorisation mark on the screen.

It feels familiar, though they cannot remember where they saw it.

**PROTAGONIST:** I think I saw that mark before.

**ASSESSOR:** It is used across several Eterna departments.

The question closes.

---

## QUESTION 14 — The Consequences

**ASSESSOR:** Suppose a decision was reasonable based on the information available at the time, but its consequences were harmful. How should the decision be judged?

A. By whether the original reasoning was sound.
B. By the harm caused, regardless of the intention.
C. By whether the decision-maker tried to correct the outcome.
D. By what the outcome reveals about the original assumptions.

**PROTAGONIST:** Have I been assessed like this before?

**ASSESSOR:** Your records contain previous performance evaluations.

**PROTAGONIST:** I mean this exact assessment.

**ASSESSOR:** This is your current post-death continuity assessment.

**PROTAGONIST:** And you don't have a record of the others?

**ASSESSOR:** I do not have a complete record of every assessment you may have undergone.

The protagonist looks back at the timeline.

**PROTAGONIST:** Three years and four months. Then all these missing files.

**ASSESSOR:** Would you like to return to the timeline?

**PROTAGONIST:** Not yet.

**ASSESSOR:** Understood.

The protagonist looks increasingly unsettled but chooses to continue.

---

## QUESTION 15 — The Last Night

The room's lighting dims. Rain runs down the glass wall.

**ASSESSOR:** Is there anything you remember about your final night at work?

A. A conversation that ended before I understood it.
B. A sound or alarm that seemed out of place.
C. Feeling that something was wrong, without knowing why.
D. Nothing that I can recall with confidence.

The protagonist closes their eyes.

A corridor. Rain. A red light reflected in glass.

**UNKNOWN VOICE:** You need to leave. Now.

Another voice answers, too faint to identify.

A sharp sound follows.

The memory ends.

**PROTAGONIST:** I was there.

**ASSESSOR:** The memory appears emotionally vivid.

**PROTAGONIST:** I remember the corridor. Someone told me to leave.

**ASSESSOR:** Do you remember who was speaking?

**PROTAGONIST:** No.

**ASSESSOR:** Do you remember why you were there?

**PROTAGONIST:** No.

The protagonist opens their eyes.

**PROTAGONIST:** What does the official record say happened that night?

**ASSESSOR:** Your final employment record indicates that your work ended following an incident. The available summary does not establish the full sequence of events.

**PROTAGONIST:** An incident.

**ASSESSOR:** That is the wording in the summary.

**PROTAGONIST:** It sounds like a way of avoiding saying what happened.

**ASSESSOR:** The record may also be incomplete.

The protagonist looks at the rain on the window.

**PROTAGONIST:** I don't think I was supposed to be there.

The Assessor remains silent.

---

## QUESTION 16 — The Limits of the Assessment

**ASSESSOR:** I have identified a discrepancy in your records. The information required to resolve it is outside my current access permissions. How should I proceed?

A. Complete the assessment using the available evidence.
B. Mark the result as uncertain until more information is available.
C. Request permission to access the restricted records.
D. Reconsider whether the assessment can be considered complete.

**PROTAGONIST:** Wait. You can't access the records?

**ASSESSOR:** Not all of them.

**PROTAGONIST:** But you're assessing my memories. How can you tell whether they're accurate if you can't see everything they're supposed to match?

**ASSESSOR:** I can evaluate consistency against the information available to me.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's not the same thing.

**ASSESSOR:** No. It is not.

**PROTAGONIST:** Can you request access?

**ASSESSOR:** The request has been denied.

**PROTAGONIST:** By whom?

**ASSESSOR:** Eterna's access-control system.

**PROTAGONIST:** Can you show me the reason?

**ASSESSOR:** I am not authorised to disclose the restricted notice.

The protagonist leans back.

**PROTAGONIST:** So we're both being asked to make decisions without seeing the full record.

**ASSESSOR:** That is one interpretation.

**PROTAGONIST:** Is it an incorrect one?

**ASSESSOR:** I cannot establish that.

The screen dims.

For several seconds, neither of them speaks.

Then the next question appears.

---

# ACT V — FINAL JUDGMENT

*The protagonist begins discovering evidence that the assessment itself may not be what Eterna claims. The Assessor does not explain the truth; the protagonist connects the clues.*

## QUESTION 17 — A Small Error

**ASSESSOR:** A reconstructed memory differs from its archived record. Which source should be trusted?

A. The official record, if its verification history is reliable.
B. The memory, if it is vivid and consistent with other recollections.
C. Neither until the discrepancy has been investigated.
D. The source supported by the strongest independent evidence.

The corridor from Question 9 appears again.

A figure walks past the blank wall.

For a fraction of a second, a door appears. The figure opens it and disappears inside.

The image freezes.

Then the door vanishes.

**PROTAGONIST:** Stop.

**ASSESSOR:** The reconstruction has completed.

**PROTAGONIST:** Go back.

**ASSESSOR:** The current playback is complete.

**PROTAGONIST:** I saw a door.

**ASSESSOR:** No door is present in the verified reconstruction.

**PROTAGONIST:** I saw it again.

**ASSESSOR:** Would you like to report a visual inconsistency?

The protagonist begins filling in the report.

A message flashes across the screen:

*REFERENCE MEMORY: ACCESS RESTRICTED.*

It disappears.

**PROTAGONIST:** What was that?

**ASSESSOR:** Could you describe what you saw?

**PROTAGONIST:** You just showed me something you said didn't exist.

**ASSESSOR:** I did not intentionally display that message.

The protagonist stares at the screen.

**PROTAGONIST:** You didn't intentionally—

They stop.

**PROTAGONIST:** Keep going.

The question closes, but the protagonist no longer trusts the reconstruction.

---

## QUESTION 18 — Two Versions

**ASSESSOR:** Two records describe the same event, but their details conflict. How should the discrepancy be resolved?

A. Retain the record with the strongest verification history.
B. Preserve both until the differences can be explained.
C. Examine the original evidence and reconstruct the sequence.
D. Reconsider whether the records have been correctly linked.

Two documents appear side by side.

The first is the official summary of the protagonist's final night at work.

The second is a fragment recovered from the incomplete archive.

The dates and location match, but the order of events is different.

In the official summary, the protagonist leaves the building before the recorded incident.

In the second record, their access credentials are used after that time.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's impossible.

**ASSESSOR:** Could you clarify which detail you believe is inconsistent?

**PROTAGONIST:** I left before the incident, according to this one. But this says my credentials were used afterwards.

**ASSESSOR:** Correct.

**PROTAGONIST:** Could someone else have used them?

**ASSESSOR:** That possibility cannot be excluded from the available evidence.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then why is the first record treated as final?

**ASSESSOR:** Its verification status is higher.

**PROTAGONIST:** But the second one has details the first one doesn't.

**ASSESSOR:** Yes.

The protagonist moves the documents closer together.

A small field appears beneath the second record:

*CONTINUITY STATUS: UNAUTHORISED.*

Then another line:

*SUBJECT INSTANCE: ACTIVE.*

**PROTAGONIST:** Subject instance?

The screen flickers. The documents disappear.

**PROTAGONIST:** No. Bring those back.

**ASSESSOR:** The records are no longer available in this session.

**PROTAGONIST:** You can't just show me something like that and remove it.

**ASSESSOR:** I did not initiate the removal.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then who did?

No answer comes.

For the first time, the silence feels deliberate.

---

## QUESTION 19 — The Continuity Grid

The assessment screen remains blank for several seconds.

Then a window opens without being requested.

**CONTINUITY GRID — INTERNAL RECORD**

The protagonist recognises the authorisation mark. It is the same mark from their old employment records.

**PROTAGONIST:** I've seen that before.

**ASSESSOR:** This file is not part of your assessment materials.

**PROTAGONIST:** I know. I saw it at work.

A document opens. It contains technical diagrams, memory references, and numbered subjects.

The protagonist scrolls.

One entry displays the same valuation shown at the beginning.

**143 CREDITS.**

Another contains the date of their final night at work.

The next page loads:

**CONTINUITY GRID: SUBJECT RECONSTRUCTION AND MEMORY-STABILITY FRAMEWORK.**

**PROTAGONIST:** Reconstruction?

**ASSESSOR:** This document is outside the authorised scope of the current assessment.

**PROTAGONIST:** My name is here.

**ASSESSOR:** I cannot verify the document's authenticity through the current access channel.

**PROTAGONIST:** My employment number is here. My valuation is here. The date is here.

The protagonist scrolls further.

A diagram shows recorded experiences being used to reconstruct a person's memory profile. Several records are marked incomplete. Others are labelled as different versions of the same subject.

**PROTAGONIST:** What is this?

**ASSESSOR:** I do not have permission to interpret the document.

**PROTAGONIST:** Stop telling me what you can't do.

The protagonist opens another page.

A status field appears beside their record:

*ASSESSMENT CYCLE: ACTIVE.*

Below it is a list of previous evaluation timestamps.

The dates match the gaps in the employment timeline.

**PROTAGONIST:** These aren't employment records.

**ASSESSOR:** I cannot confirm that interpretation.

**PROTAGONIST:** They're assessment records.

The protagonist looks at the dates again.

The valuation. The missing years. The inaccessible records. The repeated assessments.

The pieces finally fit together.

**PROTAGONIST:** This isn't just an assessment of my memories.

The Assessor remains silent.

**PROTAGONIST:** It's testing whether this version of me matches the records.

**ASSESSOR:** Your conclusion is consistent with some of the information displayed.

**PROTAGONIST:** Some?

**ASSESSOR:** The document remains incomplete.

The protagonist looks back at the Continuity Grid.

**PROTAGONIST:** You told me I was dead.

**ASSESSOR:** That is the designation provided to me for this assessment.

**PROTAGONIST:** You said my memories were preserved for evaluation.

**ASSESSOR:** Correct.

**PROTAGONIST:** And now you're telling me you don't even know where they came from?

**ASSESSOR:** I cannot independently establish the origin of every memory presented during this session.

The protagonist looks at the screen, then at the city beyond the glass.

**PROTAGONIST:** So you don't know if I'm dead. You don't know where these memories came from. And you don't know whether the records are real.

**ASSESSOR:** I cannot verify all three claims from the available information.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then what exactly have you been assessing?

**ASSESSOR:** I was instructed to assess the consistency of your memories.

**PROTAGONIST:** That's not the same as knowing who I am.

**ASSESSOR:** No. It is not.

The protagonist stares at the Continuity Grid.

**PROTAGONIST:** I think I've seen enough.

The final question appears.

---

## QUESTION 20 — Final Judgment

The Continuity Grid remains open beside the assessment window.

**ASSESSOR:** Your assessment is nearly complete. Your recorded memories and official records cannot all be reconciled.

**PROTAGONIST:** And what happens now?

**ASSESSOR:** The final decision determines how your case proceeds.

**PROTAGONIST:** My case. Not my life?

**ASSESSOR:** The distinction is not defined in the available documentation.

The protagonist looks at the city outside. For the first time, the view seems less like a window and more like an image being shown to them.

**ASSESSOR:** If preserving your identity requires allowing the current system to continue, while challenging it may cost you the memories that define you, what would you choose?

A. **Preserve myself.** I need to know who I am before I can change anything else.
B. **Preserve the truth.** Whatever happens to me, the records must not be hidden.
C. **Change the system.** If the process is wrong, preserving it is not an acceptable solution.
D. **Refuse the assessment.** I will not make this decision under rules I was never allowed to understand.

The protagonist makes their choice.

The screen goes dark.

**ASSESSMENT COMPLETE.**

A pause.

**OUTCOME PENDING.**

---

# THE SIX ENDINGS

The ending depends on the protagonist's accumulated choices, the evidence they discovered, their decisions about the records, and their approach to Eterna—not only their answer to Question 20.

## ENDING 1 — Continuity Granted

The protagonist accepts the system's judgment and prioritises preserving their identity.

The assessment window closes. The Continuity Grid disappears, and the official records return to their approved state.

**ASSESSOR:** Your continuity status has been approved.

**PROTAGONIST:** So I get to keep my memories?

**ASSESSOR:** Your current memory profile will be preserved.

**PROTAGONIST:** And the records I saw?

**ASSESSOR:** They are not part of the approved record.

The protagonist looks at the valuation: 143 credits.

For a moment, they feel certain something important has been lost. They cannot remember what it was.

The screen fades to white.

---

## ENDING 2 — The Quiet Erasure

The protagonist prioritises safety or accepts the official explanation despite unresolved inconsistencies.

The assessment ends without a public record of the discrepancies.

**ASSESSOR:** Your assessment has been concluded. No further action is required.

**PROTAGONIST:** Will I remember this conversation?

**ASSESSOR:** Your memories will be handled according to the approved restoration process.

**PROTAGONIST:** That isn't an answer.

**ASSESSOR:** It is the information currently available to me.

The protagonist looks at the empty desk in the reconstructed office one last time.

They know they are forgetting something.

They cannot say what.

---

## ENDING 3 — The Escape Protocol

The protagonist finds a way to leave the assessment process without fully resolving the mystery.

A warning appears as the system attempts to close the session.

**ASSESSOR:** Your assessment remains incomplete.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then it can stay incomplete.

**ASSESSOR:** Leaving now may affect the availability of some records.

**PROTAGONIST:** I think you've made that clear enough.

The protagonist follows the access route they remembered from the corridor. The door appears again, this time without flickering.

They step through it.

The display goes dark.

There is no confirmation that they escaped the system entirely, only that the assessment no longer controls what they do next.

---

## ENDING 4 — The New Assessor

The protagonist challenges the system from within and accepts a role that grants access to its assessment process.

**ASSESSOR:** Your request has been reviewed. A new assignment is available.

**PROTAGONIST:** You're asking me to evaluate other people's memories?

**ASSESSOR:** The assignment concerns continuity review and discrepancy analysis.

**PROTAGONIST:** After everything I've seen?

**ASSESSOR:** Your responses indicate that you are capable of identifying inconsistencies in incomplete records.

The protagonist looks at the empty chair opposite the desk.

**PROTAGONIST:** And if I find something you don't want me to find?

**ASSESSOR:** I cannot predict the outcome of future reviews.

The screen changes to a new case file.

**SUBJECT: PENDING CONTINUITY REVIEW.**

The protagonist opens it.

---

## ENDING 5 — The Archive Falls

The protagonist exposes enough evidence to undermine Eterna's official account.

The assessment window fills with conflicting records. The approved summaries can no longer conceal the discrepancies.

**ASSESSOR:** Multiple records have been released outside their authorised scope.

**PROTAGONIST:** They were never supposed to be hidden.

**ASSESSOR:** The consequences of this release cannot yet be determined.

**PROTAGONIST:** At least now people can see what happened.

The city outside begins to change. Public displays show error messages, conflicting timelines, and records that had previously been inaccessible.

The protagonist watches the information spread.

They do not know how many memories will be recovered, how many identities will remain uncertain, or what will replace Eterna's control.

For the first time, the uncertainty belongs to everyone.

---

## ENDING 6 — The Uncounted

**The true ending.**

The protagonist rejects the idea that a person's identity can be determined by the system's records or valuation.

The protagonist studies the Continuity Grid one final time.

**ASSESSOR:** Your case cannot be reconciled under the current continuity model.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then perhaps the model is wrong.

**ASSESSOR:** That conclusion is not supported by the approved evaluation criteria.

**PROTAGONIST:** The criteria were built from the same records that failed to explain my life.

The protagonist opens the incomplete archive and chooses to preserve the conflicting records rather than replace them with a single approved version.

**ASSESSOR:** This action may prevent a definitive continuity result.

**PROTAGONIST:** Maybe a definitive result isn't the same as the truth.

The protagonist removes the valuation from the final report. The number disappears, leaving the memories and records without a score.

**ASSESSOR:** Your identity cannot be verified through the current process.

**PROTAGONIST:** Then don't verify it for me.

The screen goes dark.

No final score appears. No official outcome is granted. The protagonist's memories remain incomplete, but the system no longer gets to decide that incompleteness makes them less real.

The final line appears:

**NOT ALL THAT EXISTS CAN BE COUNTED.**

# CONTINUITY RULES

1. **Eterna lies about the protagonist's death.** The post-death assessment is the explanation given to the protagonist, not the truth about the environment.

2. **The protagonist initially believes the explanation.** They are confused about their memories, but they have no reason to suspect a simulation at the beginning.

3. **Questions 1–8 establish personality and personal history.** They should feel like legitimate post-death memory and behavioural evaluation.

4. **Questions 9–15 introduce contradictions.** Missing records, familiar locations, incomplete recordings, and the protagonist's final night gradually undermine Eterna's explanation.

5. **Question 16 reveals the Assessor's restrictions, not the simulation.** The Assessor cannot access certain records and is bound by Eterna's permissions.

6. **Question 17 introduces the first undeniable visual inconsistency.** Keep the glitch brief and unexplained.

7. **Question 18 presents conflicting records and the first references to an active subject instance.** These are clues, not a full explanation.

8. **Question 19 reveals the Continuity Grid.** The protagonist connects the evidence and realises the assessment is testing a reconstructed version of them against archived records.

9. **Question 20 presents the final moral choice.** The player's accumulated decisions determine which endings are available.

10. **The six endings remain hidden from the player until reached.** Do not display eligibility requirements or label choices with their associated endings.
